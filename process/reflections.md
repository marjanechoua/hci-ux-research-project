# Reflection

## 1. Project Overview

This project focused on designing and evaluating a personal finance application with an emphasis on emotional interaction, habit tracking, and intuitive financial overview.

The development process followed an iterative approach, starting with paper prototyping and usability testing before moving toward implementation planning.

---

## 2. What Worked Well

One of the strongest aspects of the project was the early validation through paper prototypes.

Key successes included:

- Fast identification of usability issues before development
- Clear understanding of core user flows (calendar, input, overview, forecast)
- Successful integration of emotional elements into financial tracking
- Iterative improvement based on real user feedback

The combination of structured test cases and qualitative observation helped uncover both functional and conceptual misunderstandings.

---

## 3. Key Learnings

### UX Design & User Behavior

It became clear that users rely heavily on familiar mental models. Even small inconsistencies in naming or iconography can lead to confusion.

For example:

- The term “price” was often misinterpreted for income-related inputs
- The forecast icon (pig symbol) was not immediately understood without context

This reinforced the importance of:

- clear labeling
- consistent interaction patterns
- progressive disclosure of features

---

### Importance of Iteration

The iterative testing process was essential. Many issues were not visible during initial design but emerged during real interaction with paper prototypes.

Each iteration improved:

- navigation clarity
- system feedback (pop-ups)
- overall task completion success

---

### Emotional Design

Integrating emotional tagging into financial tracking proved to be a meaningful concept. Users were able to relate expenses to emotions, which added an additional layer of reflection beyond standard budgeting tools.
However, this also required careful design to avoid cognitive overload. During testing, we realised that not all users were able to easily connect their emotions with financial decisions. Our initial assumption was that users would naturally engage in this type of self-reflection, but this proved to be an oversimplification. This highlighted how design decisions can unintentionally favor users with certain levels of self-awareness while overlooking others.
Furthermore, the app implicitly promotes values such as self-reflection, emotional awareness, and personal responsibility. While these values align with the project's goals, they are not neutral. By encouraging users to examine the emotional reasons behind spending behavior, the design suggests that such reflection is beneficial and desirable.

---

## 4. Challenges

Several challenges appeared during the project:

- Designing a system that is both functional and emotionally engaging
- Balancing simplicity with feature richness
- Ensuring all icons and metaphors are universally understandable
- Translating complex financial concepts into accessible UI elements
- Reflecting on the ethical implications of design decisions and embedded values
- Ensuring the application remains inclusive for users with different levels of self-reflection and financial literacy

Especially the interpretation of icons and system feedback showed how easily assumptions from designers can differ from user expectations.

---

## 5. Final Outcome

The final prototype structure provides a clear and consistent user experience with:

- intuitive navigation between core features
- structured data input flows
- visual financial overview through calendar and charts
- emotional context integration for expenses

Most usability issues identified during testing were successfully addressed through design adjustments.

---

## 6. Personal Reflection

This project highlighted the importance of designing with users rather than for users. While usability testing helped improve navigation and interaction design, it also revealed deeper issues related to assumptions, values, and inclusivity.
Throughout the project, we became more aware that technology is never completely neutral. Design choices such as categorising purchases, encouraging emotional reflection, or deciding which inputs are mandatory all communicate certain values and influence user behaviour.

The project also demonstrated how easily designers can make assumptions about users. We initially expected users to be comfortable reflecting on the emotional causes of their spending, but testing showed that this was not always the case.
This experience reinforced the importance of considering diverse user perspectives and avoiding designs that unintentionally exclude certain groups.
Overall, the experience strengthened my understanding of user-centred design, iterative development, and the ethical responsibility designers have when shaping digital experiences.
