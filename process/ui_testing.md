# Testing & Evaluation

This section documents the evaluation of the paper prototype through structured usability testing. The goal was to validate core interaction flows, identify usability issues, and refine the interface design.

---

## 1. Test Setup

The prototype was evaluated using scenario-based usability tests with four participants representing different user stereotypes.

The tests focused on:

- navigation and orientation
- data entry (income & expenses)
- calendar interaction
- search and filtering
- forecasting and insights
- system feedback (pop-ups)

---

## 2. Test Cases Overview

A total of 13 test cases were defined to evaluate key interaction flows:

- Calendar navigation (monthly / weekly view)
- Date-based transaction inspection
- Expense creation and categorization
- Recurring payments
- Search functionality
- Forecast interpretation
- Emotion-based expense tagging
- Account switching and overview understanding

Each test case was linked to specific research objectives (E1–E9), ensuring structured evaluation of usability goals.

---

## 3. Research Objectives

The evaluation focused on the following usability aspects:

- **E1:** Understanding of navigation structure
- **E2:** Interaction with calendar dates
- **E3:** Correct creation of income and expenses
- **E4:** Understanding of financial terminology
- **E5:** Triggering and understanding of system feedback (pop-ups)
- **E6:** Independent discovery of forecast and balance features
- **E7:** Emotional tagging of expenses
- **E8:** Filtering and search behavior
- **E9:** Switching between accounts

---

## 4. Key Findings

The overall navigation concept was well understood by all participants after initial exploration.

### Positive outcomes:

- Users were able to complete most tasks without assistance after initial orientation.
- Calendar interaction and account overview were intuitive after short familiarization.
- Emotion tagging was well received and understood.

### Identified usability issues:

- Some icons (e.g. forecast symbol) were not immediately understood.
- Terminology such as “price” caused confusion and was too abstract.
- Certain pop-ups (especially in the calendar flow) were unclear in their purpose.
- Interactive elements were not always visually obvious.

---

## 5. Stereotype Comparison

Two user groups were compared:

- Users with stronger financial knowledge
- Users with higher technical affinity but lower financial experience

**Result:**  
Both groups showed a similar number of usability issues, indicating that the interface is generally balanced across different user types.

---

## 6. Design Adjustments

Based on the test results, several improvements were made:

- Improved navigation clarity (icon separation and structure)
- Renamed ambiguous labels:
  - “Price” → “Amount”
  - “Title” → “Description”
  - “Feelings” → “Emotions”
  - “Type” → “Category”
- Reduced confusion in pop-up flows by simplifying interaction paths
- Improved visibility of interactive elements
- Refined forecast representation (pig icon clarification and onboarding)

---

## 7. Conclusion

The usability tests confirmed that the core concept of the application is intuitive and understandable across different user groups.

Most issues were related to visual clarity and terminology rather than structural problems.

The iterative testing process was essential in identifying usability gaps early and improving the overall user experience before implementation.
