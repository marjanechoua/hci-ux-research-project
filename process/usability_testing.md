# Usability Testing of the Implemented Application

## Purpose

After implementation, the application was evaluated through usability testing with external participants.

The goal was to assess:

- task completion
- navigation efficiency
- feature discoverability
- understanding of the application's purpose
- accessibility and language comprehension

---

## Method

The evaluation was conducted using a think-aloud protocol.

Participants were asked to complete realistic tasks based on the two project stereotypes:

- Philip (impulsive spender)
- Christof (structured planner)

A moderator guided the session while an observer documented behavior, difficulties, and completion times.

---

## Example Tasks

### Philip

- Check the number of impulse purchases this month
- Record a new impulse purchase and assign an emotion
- Determine whether another purchase would exceed the monthly budget
- Identify categories with the highest number of impulse purchases

### Christof

- Check current account balance
- Review previous supermarket expenses
- Create an account for a family member
- Add a recurring monthly subscription
- Determine the projected balance at the end of the month

---

## Key Findings

### Positive Findings

- Core financial functions were generally understandable.
- Calendar navigation was easy to learn.
- Expense entry could be completed efficiently.
- Emotional tagging was understood after short exploration.

### Identified Issues

- The purpose of the forecasting feature was not immediately clear.
- Some navigation paths required trial and error.
- Account creation was difficult to discover.
- Language barriers significantly affected usability for one participant.
- The overall purpose of the application required clearer communication.

---

## Design Implications

The results suggested improvements in:

- onboarding and feature explanation
- navigation clarity
- discoverability of account management features
- terminology and language accessibility
- visual guidance throughout the application

---

## Reflection

The evaluation demonstrated that usability is influenced not only by interface design but also by language proficiency, prior experience, and understanding of the application's underlying concept.

This highlighted the importance of accessibility and inclusive design when developing reflective financial applications.
