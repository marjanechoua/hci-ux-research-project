# Survey Results — Quantitative User Study

This document presents the results of the quantitative survey conducted as part of the Refy HCI research process.

The goal of the survey was to collect data about financial behavior, digital payment habits, and contextual factors influencing spending decisions.

---

## 1. Dataset Overview

A total of **16 participants** took part in the survey.

The dataset includes a diverse range of participants in terms of:

- age
- income level
- occupation
- living situation
- digital payment behavior
- financial management experience

---

## 2. Collected Variables

The survey captured the following dimensions:

### Demographic Data

- Age
- Occupation
- Living situation
- Income level

### Behavioral Data

- Frequency of cashless payments
- Preferred payment methods
- Typical purchases
- Time pressure level

### Self-Reported Attributes

- Financial management experience
- Digital device proficiency
- Personal self-description

---

## 3. Data Cleaning Notes

- Some entries contained incomplete or inconsistent responses (e.g., missing hobbies or financial experience fields).
- Duplicate participants appeared in a few cases (e.g., repeated responses from the same participant with slight variation).
- These entries were kept for behavioral pattern analysis but normalized during interpretation.

---

## 4. General Distribution Patterns

### 4.1 Age Distribution

- Majority of participants are between **18–32 years old**
- Smaller subgroup of participants aged **50–59**

---

### 4.2 Income Distribution

- Most participants fall into:
  - < 500€
  - 500€ – 1000€
  - > 800€ (self-reported mixed category)

- Higher income (> 2000€) appears mostly in older participants

---

### 4.3 Payment Behavior

- Most frequently used payment methods:
  - Debit card
  - Apple / Google Pay
  - PayPal / Klarna (secondary usage)

- Cash usage is rare and mostly limited to specific cases

---

### 4.4 Frequency of Cashless Payments

- Daily or frequent usage is dominant among younger participants
- Older participants show more moderate usage patterns

---

### 4.5 Time Pressure

- Most participants report medium to high time pressure (3–5 scale)
- Only a small subset reports low time pressure

---

## 5. Behavioral Observations (Descriptive Only)

### 5.1 Spending Categories

Most common spending category:

- food / groceries
- lifestyle consumption (music, sports, entertainment)

---

### 5.2 Financial Awareness

- Some participants actively track finances using apps or personal strategies
- Others rely mainly on intuition or banking apps

---

### 5.3 Digital Payment Dependency

- Mobile payments are widely adopted among younger users
- Cash usage correlates with higher perceived financial awareness

---

## 6. Emerging Patterns (No Interpretation)

The dataset shows variation along the following dimensions:

- structured vs. unstructured financial behavior
- high vs. low financial awareness
- impulsive vs. planned spending tendencies
- digital vs. traditional payment preferences

---

## 7. Role of This Dataset in the Project

The survey data was used as input for:

- clustering analysis (Birch + Isolation Forest)
- stereotype generation (Philip & Christof)
- interview participant selection
- persona development
- requirement definition

---

## 8. Key Output

This dataset provides a behavioral foundation describing:

> how different users manage money in everyday life contexts
