# Interview Study — Emotional Financial Behavior

This document summarizes semi-structured interviews conducted to explore financial decision-making, emotional influences, and digital tool usage.

The goal of the study was to understand how users think, feel, and behave when managing personal finances.

---

## 1. Research Focus

The interviews explored four main dimensions:

- life circumstances and financial behavior
- emotional influences on spending
- online shopping and social media effects
- expectations toward digital financial tools

---

## 2. Research Hypotheses

The following hypotheses guided the interview design:

- Planning expenses increases perceived financial stability
- Digital financial tools improve overview of spending
- Emotional states influence impulsive purchases
- Financial strategies reduce regret after spending
- Budget awareness improves response to financial stress
- Social media influences younger users’ spending behavior
- Frequent online shoppers experience lower financial control
- Emotional support in financial apps increases engagement

---

## 3. Interview Method

The interviews were conducted after an initial quantitative survey analysis and clustering phase.

Based on emerging behavioral patterns in the dataset, two representative user profiles were identified:

- one structured, planning-oriented financial behavior type
- one impulsive, emotion-driven spending behavior type

Participants matching these behavioral patterns were selected for semi-structured interviews in order to further explore underlying motivations and decision-making processes.

The interviews were conducted with:

- **Christof** — structured, planning-oriented financial behavior
- **Philip** — impulsive, emotion-driven spending behavior

---

## 4. Key Behavioral Findings

### 4.1 Emotional Influence on Spending

Spending behavior is strongly influenced by emotional states such as:

- stress
- boredom
- reward-seeking behavior
- social influence

Philip showed a strong connection between emotions and impulsive purchases, while Christof exhibited more controlled behavior.

---

### 4.2 Financial Awareness and Control

A clear difference emerged in financial awareness:

- Christof actively monitors and discusses finances within the household
- Philip experiences difficulties at the end of the month and lacks consistent planning

---

### 4.3 Digital Payment Behavior

Both participants use digital payments frequently.

However:

- digital payments reduce “tangible awareness” of spending
- cash usage provides stronger spending feedback

---

### 4.4 Social Media Influence

Social media strongly affects younger users:

- Philip reported being influenced by trends and online content
- Christof reported minimal influence from social media advertising

---

### 4.5 Financial Strategies

Participants with structured strategies (e.g. planning, comparison, household discussion):

- experience fewer regrets
- feel more financial control
- respond more effectively to budget constraints

---

## 5. Hypothesis Evaluation

### H1 — Planning improves financial stability

✔ Partially confirmed  
Structured planning correlates with lower stress and higher control.

---

### H2 — Digital tools improve overview

✔ Partially confirmed  
Tools help awareness, but do not replace personal discipline.

---

### H3 — Emotions trigger impulsive spending

✔ Confirmed  
Strong emotional correlation found, especially in Philip’s case.

---

### H4 — Strategies reduce regret

✔ Confirmed  
Structured users show significantly less purchase regret.

---

### H5 — Budget awareness improves reaction to stress

✔ Confirmed  
Active monitoring improves financial responsiveness.

---

### H6 — Social media influences younger users

✔ Confirmed  
Strong effect on younger participant.

---

### H7 — Online shopping reduces control

✔ Partially confirmed  
Depends on financial experience and awareness.

---

### H8 — Emotional support increases usage

✔ Partially confirmed  
High relevance for users with financial stress.

---

## 6. Key Behavioral Differences

### Christof — Structured Planner

- family-oriented financial responsibility
- structured budgeting behavior
- low emotional influence
- strong planning strategies
- stable financial awareness

---

### Philip — Impulsive Spender

- emotion-driven purchasing behavior
- low financial stability
- frequent impulsive decisions
- social media influence
- desire for financial guidance

---

## 7. Design Implications

The interview findings directly influenced the system design:

- emotional tagging of expenses
- impulse purchase labeling
- calendar-based financial overview
- spending pattern visualization
- reflective feedback mechanisms

---

## 8. Key Insight

Financial behavior is not purely rational.

It is shaped by:

- emotions
- social influence
- digital payment behavior
- individual financial experience

Design must therefore support not only tracking, but reflection and awareness.
