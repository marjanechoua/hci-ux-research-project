# Research Insights — From Methodology to Behavioral Understanding

This document describes the research methodology, classification criteria, and conceptual foundations used to derive user understanding in the Refy project.

It connects the initial research goals with the analytical framework used to interpret survey and interview data.

---

## 1. Project Goal

The goal of this research was to support users in developing a better understanding of their financial behavior.

We aimed to:

- identify causes of misjudged spending behavior
- understand emotional and behavioral influences on financial decisions
- analyze differences in digital financial literacy
- derive implications for a user-centered financial reflection system

---

## 2. Research Methodology

The research process followed a two-stage mixed-method approach:

### 2.1 Quantitative Phase (Survey)

An online questionnaire was conducted to collect structured data on:

- financial behavior
- payment habits
- demographic background
- digital financial experience
- time pressure and lifestyle factors

The goal of this phase was to generate a dataset suitable for behavioral clustering.

---

### 2.2 Qualitative Phase (Interviews)

Based on the survey results, representative user profiles (stereotypes) were selected.

These users were interviewed in order to gain deeper insights into:

- emotional influences on spending behavior
- decision-making processes
- financial stress and coping strategies
- perception of digital financial tools

---

## 3. Research Rationale

The combination of survey and interviews was chosen to:

- capture broad behavioral patterns (survey)
- understand underlying motivations (interviews)
- validate assumptions across both data types
- reduce bias from purely qualitative or quantitative analysis

---

## 4. Classification Framework

To analyze and structure user behavior, a set of criteria was defined.

These criteria were used to categorize participants and identify behavioral patterns.

---

### 4.1 Primary Classification Criteria (High Relevance)

These criteria directly influence financial behavior:

- income level
- time pressure
- payment methods and habits
- motivation (control, saving, overview)

---

### 4.2 Secondary Classification Criteria (Supporting Factors)

These criteria support interpretation of behavior:

- living situation
- age (life stage)

---

### 4.3 Domain-Relevant Criteria (Design-Relevant)

These criteria are directly relevant for system design:

- experience with financial planning
- digital device proficiency

---

## 5. Operationalization of User Characteristics

The following dimensions were used to structure the dataset:

- age groups
- income levels
- living situations
- occupational status
- time pressure (scale-based)
- digital financial experience (scale-based)
- preferred payment methods
- frequency of cashless payments
- digital literacy

These variables enabled structured comparison and clustering of user behavior.

---

## 6. Conceptual Insight from Research Design

The chosen criteria reflect the assumption that financial behavior is influenced by:

- structural conditions (income, life situation)
- cognitive load (time pressure)
- digital capability (tool experience)
- behavioral habits (payment methods)

This framework allowed the identification of meaningful behavioral clusters within the dataset.

---

## 7. Role in the Overall Project

This research phase formed the foundation for:

- survey data analysis
- clustering and stereotype generation
- interview selection
- persona development
- requirement definition

---

## 8. Key Insight

The research approach shows that financial behavior cannot be explained by a single factor.

Instead, it emerges from the interaction of:

- economic constraints
- emotional states
- digital habits
- situational pressure

These dimensions form the conceptual basis for the Refy design system.
